Experiment notes

Sparse arrivals, fast modulation

The time between photons does not set the frequency limit. Each absolute timestamp contributes a phase to the Fourier probe. Repetition and enough detections let those phases add up, even when a conventional histogram looks noisy.

Model assumptions and paper references

Incident arrivals follow an inhomogeneous Poisson process. For a held source bin, the count is Poisson(rate × duration) and arrival times are continuous and uniform within that bin. Detection efficiency and dark events come before dead time; Gaussian jitter and timestamp rounding come afterward.

The Fourier plot probes the complete recorded stream, even while the time plots are zoomed. DC is reported separately. The CFAR threshold is the paper’s low-flux approximation; it is not calibrated for strong dead time or very few detections. Reconstruction uses DC plus twice the real part of positive-frequency coefficients, keeping at most 40 accepted probes. It is an illustrative estimate without dead-time compensation or jitter deconvolution.

Image/video values become assumed relative flux after optional approximate sRGB linearization and spatial resizing. Frames are held over their intervals. A conventional video contains no measured picosecond light transport. This input mode does not implement Transient NeRF or a lidar renderer.
